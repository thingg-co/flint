# Flint roadmap

The future-work list from the whitepaper (Section 11), in the order it is being worked. Each
item is a hypothesis for the walk-forward backtest: it ships behind a switch where it can, lands
in one commit with tests, and is judged by a backtest before and after. Items that change the
feature set reset the model once, so they are batched into a single reset on a weekend.

| 3c | Fixed costs and the ticket floor | shipped 0.5.18 | the user's catch of 2026-10-12: every cost in the edge was proportional (spread, impact, decay in bps of notional) and Kelly at 0.08–0.15 over hundreds of names sized most positions under the paper book's own $1,500 rebalance floor, so signals showed as BUY x0.01 and never filled, while a bearish view bought as fractional put contracts paid a commission that was never in the edge. Now `min_position_dollars` (1,000, a live control) lifts a smaller signal to the floor when its edge still covers the costs at that size, else it holds; a put's whole-contract commission is amortised over the hold (`max(min hold, horizon)` hours) in the edge test and shown as `ticket_bps`; the paper book fills whole contracts and never past the premium cap, and uses the same floor. | 4 | Ensemble of two 4XL networks | coded 0.5.20 behind `FLINT_MEMBERS` / `backtest_members` (default 1); a backtest with `backtest_members=2` judges it | the learner holds `members` nets that train in turn (each step is one member's own batch, so each sees 1/members of the steps) and forecast as one: quantiles, gate and attention averaged (a mean of non-crossing quantile vectors does not cross), the direction heads as the mean probability. Memory: the activations dominate a 4XL's 52.8 GB peak and the members run one after another, so a second member costs its parameters and Adam state (about 0.3 GB), not a second peak; the step costs `members` times, so warmup and the idle trainer's epochs stretch by the same factor. A one-member checkpoint seeds an ensemble (the other members start fresh) and an ensemble's first member serves a one-member run, so the switch never resets anything. The backtest runs the same ensemble and, with `backtest_members` > 1, stores the first member's own forecasts next to the ensemble's (`q0`, `up0`, `dn0`), so `flint sweep --single` replays one net against the ensemble over identical days: the judgment within one run. The 2026-10-18 weekend judges the feature batch at one member; the ensemble run follows (its halved steps per day are the price to measure) |

Baseline: the first full walk-forward backtest on the 362-name universe runs the weekend of
2026-10-10 (absolute target, 4XL, 30 h budget); its report is the "before" for everything above.

Absolute-target run, 2026-10-11 (74 scored days, 4XL, 61 steps a day, 5 bps cost): net −144 bps,
−1.9 bps a day, Sharpe −3.1, hit 51.4%, 289 signals a day, 20 winning days. What the stored
forecasts say (`flint sweep` and the notebook-style checks behind it):

- The net has a small, real cross-sectional signal: rank IC 0.032 (t = 14.5 over 5,259 bars),
  positive in seven of eight 10-day blocks, and the direction hit climbs with confidence, from
  50.3% in the least confident fifth to 53.7% in the top 1%, where the mean move in the called
  direction is 24 bps against 5 bps of cost. Only the top tenth or so clears the cost.
- The loss is the policy, not the sign: the book was 70% short (the net leans bearish, mean
  P(down) 0.44 against P(up) 0.40) through a period the equal-weight universe rose 26 bps, and the
  short leg lost about three times what the long leg made; costs took another 56 bps.
- The raw quantiles are about half as wide as the realised spread (raw 10–90 covers 37%, IQR 47
  bps against 85 realised), so the raw pinball (36.3) is worse than the last ten days' unconditional
  quantiles (30.4); the live band calibration widens them to the 80% the report shows. A 74-day
  walk-forward net at 61 steps a day is much less trained than the live model, so this is a floor.
- The sweep's proposal (score 0.8, margin 0.12, 16 calls before a name trades, Kelly 0.6) makes
  +36 bps on the tuning half on 429 trades and +7 bps on the confirming half on 20 trades: it
  found "almost never trade", which is a hint about the gates, not a setting.

Candidates this points at: a bearish-bias check in the policy (net exposure capped by the index
view), trading only the top confidence decile (meta-labeling, item 5, is the principled form),
and the feature batch (item 2a) so the net sees something other than price.

Relative-target run, 2026-10-11 (the same 74 days, the second half of the weekend): net −33 bps,
−0.5 bps a day, Sharpe −1.4, max drawdown 44 bps, 120 signals a day, hit 51.2%. The relative
target wins on every measure the stored forecasts give: rank IC 0.033 (t = 19.8), no bearish lean
(49.7% of calls up against the absolute run's 47.0%; median bias −0.1 bps against −6.4), the top 1%
of confidence calls a 22 bps mean move in the called direction at 55.3% hit (absolute: 11 bps at
53.2%), raw 10–90 coverage 40% against 37%, and the second half of the days holds every one of
those. What it lost went to the hedge: the basket scored against residual labels made +14 bps
gross and paid 20 bps of its own cost, and the index hedge paid another 28 bps, because the
five-index loadings sum to 1.5 units of hedge per unit of basket (item 3b). The current gates
hardly trade in this mode (48 of 74 days without a signal: the per-name skill gate, not the
global hit EMA, which sat below 0.5 on six of them). The sweep's proposal (score 0.8, margin 0.04,
move floor 16 bps, Kelly 0.08, confirm 1, min hit 0.45, 16 calls) trades on the net's own
confidence instead: +69 bps on the tuning half (Sharpe 3.7) and +16 bps on the confirming half
(Sharpe 1.3, 7,237 trades, 19 of 37 days up), not thin this time; before the hedge's cost it
makes +119 and +76, and priced at 1 bps on the index ETFs it nets +174 (confirm +64).
The Spark runs relative from its next start (launcher, 2026-10-11) and the hedge is priced at 1 bps
(3b); the gates are the operator's call, with the next weekend's run as the judge.
